# SimpleCare Competitor Research

Reviewed five platforms competing directly or adjacently with SimpleCare: three BC same-day virtual walk-in clinics (Walk In, Avee Health, 121Clinicians), one physician-tooling/practice platform (Rocket Doctor), and the national paid on-demand leader (Maple), for contrast. All content pulled live from each site.

## The three direct BC competitors

**Walk In** (walkin.ca) — Free with MSP, BC-only, positions itself as skipping the traditional walk-in clinic. Model: patient registers, then **picks a specific appointment time slot** (not a live queue) for phone or video, sees "any physician available," gets prescriptions/notes electronically after. No visible wait-time or queue-position feature anywhere on the marketing site.

**Avee Health** (avee.health) — Free with MSP, $49 for private (non-MSP) patients. Same slot-booking model: "Just a phone number and a time that works for you." Notably shows **next available slot directly on the homepage** ("Next time: Tomorrow, 6:15 AM") as a trust/urgency signal before you even start booking. Runs a B2B channel selling into pharmacies ("Avee for Pharmacies," 700+ BC pharmacies) to drive prescription/in-store visit volume — a distribution angle SimpleCare doesn't currently have. Real patient reviews on their own site openly mention **the doctor calling later than the scheduled window** — the exact pain point your live queue is designed to solve, sitting in their own testimonials as an accepted trade-off rather than something they've fixed.

**121Clinicians** (121clinicians.com) — Free with MSP, otherwise pay-per-service ($35 flat for a doctor's note, called out explicitly). Broadest documented scope of the three: standard cold/flu/UTI/skin visits plus **ICBC medical reporting for car accident claims** — a niche, high-intent service line none of the others mention and SimpleCare doesn't currently offer. Their own FAQ has two entries essentially apologizing for the same problem Avee's reviews mention: "my appointment time has passed but I have not been contacted" and "doctors infrequently run behind, please be patient" — this confirms it's a category-wide, unsolved weakness, not a one-off complaint.

**The pattern across all three:** none of them show a live queue, position, or real-time wait state anywhere in their patient-facing product. They're all scheduled-appointment models wearing "walk-in" branding, and all three either display or field complaints about doctors calling later than the given window with no visibility into why or how much longer. This is the single clearest opening for SimpleCare's actual live-queue UX (the waiting/next/in-progress/delayed states we built) to be a genuine differentiator rather than a "nice to have" — it's solving a documented, publicly-visible pain point none of your direct competitors have addressed.

## Adjacent players, for contrast

**Rocket Doctor** (rocketdoctor.io) — Originally Canadian, now primarily US-focused (Medi-Cal, Medicare California/NY) following an acquisition by Treatment.com AI. Relevant less as a patient-side competitor and more for the physician-tooling angle: their pitch to doctors is "helping physicians become independent again," with "smart-triage and expert matching" before a doctor is assigned, and an explicit "Enhanced Physician Support" pillar — a dedicated team plus tooling to support the physician side of the platform. Worth a look when we get further into your physician/MO portals, since it's one of the few competitors that markets its provider-side experience at all, rather than only the patient funnel.

**Maple** (getmaple.ca) — The Canadian category leader, but a structurally different product: private-pay membership ($85/month) or per-visit, chat/audio/video **on demand**, not MSP-billed and not scheduled — "care in minutes," no appointment window at all. Broader specialty bench (dermatology, mental health, pediatric, proactive/chronic care) and a B2B employer-benefits arm. Trust signals leaned on heavily: 2M+ users, 4.8 app rating, 30K+ reviews, shown prominently above the fold. Useful mainly as a reminder of how much specialty breadth and social proof matter to conversion, even though its business model (paid membership vs. MSP) isn't directly comparable to SimpleCare's.

## What this means for SimpleCare specifically

The live queue with real states is a legitimate, evidenced differentiator — lean into it rather than treating it as just an internal UX nicety; it's solving a complaint your competitors' own patients are posting publicly. Consider surfacing a "next available" signal on the marketing/landing side the way Avee does, since it functions as both a trust signal and a conversion nudge before someone even starts booking. ICBC medical reporting (121Clinicians) is a discrete, high-intent service line worth evaluating as a potential addition — it's a real revenue/traffic source none of the others besides 121Clinicians mention. On chronic care, Maple's breadth (dedicated pediatric/mental-health/chronic/proactive categories) is worth benchmarking against the two-condition chronic care screen we prototyped — there's room to grow that surface. And worth watching Rocket Doctor specifically for physician-side ideas (smart-triage, dedicated physician support tooling) as we move into the physician and MO portals, since it's the only competitor here that visibly invests in the provider experience at all.
