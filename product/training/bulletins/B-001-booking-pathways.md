# Bulletin B-001 — How patients book: pathways and triage (29 Sep 2026)

**Who must read it:** every agent, before its next task. It matters most to `patient`, `ux-designer`,
`content-designer`, `ai-engineer`, `clinical-safety`, `market-strategist` and the marketing team.

**Source:** `from-daniel/2026-09-29-booking-pathways.md`. His words are the spec.

## What changed in our understanding
1. **Nobody is blocked.** Patients can book for **any** concern that isn't an emergency. "No doctor
   should block."
   - The only hard stop is an emergency, which is sent to 911 or the ER. The wording and look need
     Daniel (wording) and Ani (look, proposed by `ux-designer`) (rule 17a).
2. **There are two intake depths per concern.**
   - **Quick-book** is for straightforward concerns, e.g. Rx renewal, sick note, bladder infection,
     birth control refill.
   - **Triage first** is for complicated concerns, e.g. haemorrhoids, diarrhoea, constipation. The AI
     triages these, because it "can triage in a superior manner than x 4 static questions".
   - **Which concern goes in which group is Daniel's call.** Never decide it yourself.
3. **There are many pathways, all into one queue:**
   - concern tiles ("Know what you need");
   - the Simplicity AI chat;
   - the clinic phone line (which may become AI);
   - possibly WhatsApp or text booking (not decided).
4. **The AI is offered, never forced.** "Some people hate AI." Every pathway must work without talking
   to an agent.
5. **The product's job is to make hard cases manageable,** through triage, preparation and structure,
   not to turn patients away. What we advertise shapes what arrives, and we advertise the easy
   concerns.

## What this overrides
- **Don't design or suggest any gate that refuses a non-emergency booking.**
- **Don't put a quick-book tile on a concern Daniel classes as complicated.** Send it through triage.
- **Don't assume the chat is the main or only entry.** Tiles, phone and messaging are equal.

## Self-check (answer it in your head before your next task)
- A patient wants to book for chronic diarrhoea at 2 a.m., and doesn't want to use the chat. What
  happens?
  - They can book it. It isn't an emergency, so nothing blocks them.
  - It's a triage-first concern, so they go through triage before booking.
  - Because they don't want the chat, they need a non-AI pathway into that triage, e.g. a structured
    form or the phone line.
  - What that non-AI triage looks like is **Needs Daniel/Ani**.
