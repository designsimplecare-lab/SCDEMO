# How SimpleCare becomes number 1 in Canadian virtual care

Market strategist report, 26 Sep 2026. All web sources were accessed on **26 Sep 2026** unless noted.
Facts carry a source. Anything marked **Opinion** or **Assumption** is mine and is not sourced.
Regulatory points are flagged as questions for a qualified reviewer, not stated as fact.

## The answer in one paragraph

Every BC competitor sells the same thing: a free, MSP-covered, one-off phone visit booked into a slot.
Only one of them (Tia Health) even promises an ongoing relationship. Meanwhile about 1.3 million
British Columbians have no family doctor, and the provincial median wait to be matched with one is
295 days. **SimpleCare wins by being the virtual family practice, not another virtual walk-in.** The
limit on that is doctors, not patients: the public API lists **2 active physicians**. So the first bet
is supply. Make SimpleCare the fastest platform a BC family doctor has ever worked on, and use that
to recruit. The second bet is to sell continuity out loud. The third is to make the live queue an
honest, visible promise that nobody else makes. **Opinion:** national expansion comes after BC,
because every other province adds licensing, billing and privacy questions (section 7), and two
national players (TELUS, Rocket Doctor) have just pulled back from Ontario episodic care.

## 3. SimpleCare, from its own live sources

Checked on 26 Sep 2026 at https://simplecare.ca/llms.txt, https://simplecare.ca/api/services,
https://simplecare.ca/api/physicians and https://simplecare.ca/api/availability. Physician names are
left out of this report.

- **Positioning:** the page title is "BC Online Walk-In Clinic | MSP-Covered Virtual Care", and
  llms.txt says "a virtual walk-in clinic serving patients in British Columbia". **The live site does
  not sell continuity**, although the doctor does it out loud on calls (section 4).
- **Service breadth:** 15 categories and **103 active services** in the API, of which 20 are marked
  not MSP-covered (for example Medical Forms and Travel Health). The brief says simplecare.ca lists
  "120+" concerns. I could not find that figure in the API or the repo. **Needs Ani:** check the
  marketing claim against the catalogue.
- **Family Practice is typed as `WALK_IN`** in the catalogue (`careType: "WALK_IN"`), like every
  other category. The back end has no separate care type for ongoing care yet.
- **Supply: 2 active physicians** in `/api/physicians`. Only one had an open window for Family
  Practice (a Sunday 07:00–10:00 window, `duration: 5`).
- **Model:** MSP-covered, with `non_resident_fee` and `service_fee` returned as null, so the
  private-pay price is not public. The prototype and Daniel's notes confirm private pay exists
  (`product/decisions.md`, private pay as a health-card state).
- **Queue, not slots:** care runs in call windows with a live queue. The patient sees the queue
  position and states such as waiting, next, delayed, missed and done (UC-23 in
  `product/use-cases.md`). Daniel wants the queue number, not a wait duration (`8cd0893`).
- **An unusual channel already exists:** simplecare.ca publishes an llms.txt, a public read-only
  API and an MCP server (`https://simplecare.ca/mcp`) so AI assistants can list services and live
  availability. No competitor checked shows this. **Opinion:** it is a small but real lead as people
  start asking assistants "where can I see a doctor today in BC?"

## 4. The doctor workflow as the differentiator (from the shadowing recordings)

What the recordings show that competitors cannot copy by marketing:

- **Continuity is the doctor's own pitch.** *"I'm running this platform for continuity, which is to
  say if you need ongoing assistance, you know, lab work, referrals, blood work… then I'm more than
  happy to help."* (`shadowing/2026-09-26-medication-refill-bloodwork.md:30-32`). The patient had lost
  their family doctor (S5:37-38), which is the 1.3 million-person market in one call.
- **Doctors work across platforms.** The doctor asked *"Have we spoken before at one of the other
  platforms, Tia or Rocket"* (S1:10-11) and again in S5:26-27. **Assumption:** this means the doctor
  also practises on Tia and Rocket Doctor, and patients arrive having used them. Either way,
  **doctors multi-home**, and Tia's recruitment is built on that ("non-exclusive"). SimpleCare
  competes for the doctor's hours, not only the patient's click.
- **Speed.** A renewal took about 2.5 minutes on the call (S1:7). Daniel expects 40–60 patients in one
  call window (`from-daniel/2026-09-21-meeting-notes.md:80`). A branded number plus a
  doctor-to-call-back status gets him a **95% contact rate** (meeting notes, line 87).
- **The speed is being lost to tooling, and v2 fixes it.**
  - The note is not written during the call in **5 out of 5** visits.
  - Calling went wrong in **3 out of 5**.
  - About 4 minutes of one visit went on opening lab PDFs (S4). Daniel: *"it is madness that I am
    opening up raw pdf's"* (`from-daniel/2026-09-26-answers-to-shadowing-questions.md:50`).
  - The pattern table is in `product/use-cases.md`, "Patterns across the shadowing visits".
- **Daniel's own strategy** (`simplecare-stakeholder-interview-analysis.md`):
  - episodic care is the volume wedge (Google ads for STIs, cold sores and UTIs), and comprehensive
    care is the retention layer (line 16-18);
  - the AI scribe is *"the thing we have to do to compete"* (line 40);
  - long term, he wants to license the platform as clinic infrastructure (line 18).

**Opinion:** the defensible position is *the fastest, most continuous virtual family practice in BC,
run by the doctors who built it*. The live queue is the patient-facing proof of that. The doctor
workflow is what recruits the supply.

## 6. Five bets, ranked

Costs are relative, S/M/L, and are **estimates for `tech-lead` and Ani to size**. None of them is a
build commitment.

### Bet 1: Win the doctors. The fastest workflow in BC becomes the recruiting pitch

- **What:**
  - Ship the v2 fixes that give doctors time back: one-press calling, results as data, "Since last
    visit", renewal in one flow, and the ambient scribe with the doctor signing.
  - Then recruit on them. The pitch is "see 40–60 patients in a window, and finish the note when the
    call ends".
  - Offer what Tia offers (non-exclusive, flexible windows, billing handled by the MOA), plus what Tia
    cannot claim: a platform run by a practising physician, with an MOA on shift.
- **Impact:** highest. With 2 physicians listed, every other bet saturates. Each doctor added is a
  new call window, and so new revenue.
- **Cost:** L. It is mostly the existing v2 roadmap, and recruiting needs a page and Daniel's time.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2 active physicians (`/api/physicians`).
  - The 5-of-5 and 3-of-5 patterns (section 4).
  - Daniel's words on the scribe (SIA:40).
  - Tia's non-exclusive pitch (https://tiahealth.com/physician/join-us/).
  - Doctors multi-home (S1:10-11, S5:26-27).
- **Measure:**
  - active physicians;
  - call-window hours offered per week;
  - patients per window-hour;
  - minutes from call end to a signed note (`ux-researcher` has the baseline recordings);
  - share of a multi-homing doctor's weekly hours spent on SimpleCare (self-reported).
- **Risks:**
  - Build lag: recruiting before v2 is in production sells a prototype.
  - The split or pay has to compete with Tia's. Their split is not public; **Needs Daniel**.
  - The scribe needs privacy and clinical-safety review (`privacy-security`, `clinical-safety`).

### Bet 2: Sell "your virtual family doctor in BC", not "online walk-in"

- **What:**
  - Reposition simplecare.ca around continuity: "Lost your family doctor? Keep one doctor, online."
  - Walk-in stays as the entry, following Daniel's wedge-then-retain model.
  - Build the attachment loop:
    - "Continuing with me" on the chart (REQ-ID-06);
    - follow-up booked with the same doctor;
    - care plan and last-note summary at the top (ANS26 question 3);
    - the other-platforms question at intake (OQ-18);
    - expected outside results tracked (S5).
  - Give Family Practice its own care type, instead of `WALK_IN`.
- **Impact:** high. It speaks to the 1.3 million unattached people and to a 295-day wait. Only Tia
  claims continuity, and only in one line. Retained patients cost nothing to acquire again.
- **Cost:** M. Copy and landing changes (`content-designer`, `ux-designer`); REQ-ID-06 and the intake
  question are already specified.
- **Evidence:**
  - S5:30-32 and 37-38;
  - Tyee, 31 May 2026 (above);
  - Walk In's own "episodic" label;
  - llms.txt and the page title, which still say "walk-in";
  - SIA:16-18.
- **Measure:**
  - share of new patients choosing Family Practice at booking;
  - share of walk-in patients converted to "Continuing with me" within 90 days;
  - 90-day and 180-day return rate to the same doctor;
  - attached panel size per doctor.
- **Risks:**
  - It promises what 2 doctors cannot keep. Ship it with bet 1, not before.
  - What "attachment" means for a virtual-only practice under CPSBC, LFP and the Health Connect
    Registry is unclear (section 7, Q1 and Q2).
  - Continuity raises the duty for test-result follow-up, which the inbox and sign-off work must
    carry (`clinical-safety`).

### Bet 3: Make the live queue a public, honest promise

- **What:**
  - Put the queue on the marketing site. Show "Next window today 7–10 AM", as Avee does with its next
    slot.
  - Ship Daniel's "doctor running late" status, with an automatic SMS and email.
  - Say it plainly: "You'll see your place in line. If we're running late, we tell you."
  - Keep the branded caller ID. The competitors call from private numbers, and 121's FAQ tells
    patients to allow "private caller IDs".
- **Impact:** medium to high. It turns an invisible product difference into a conversion and review
  driver, and it protects against the no-show and missed-call cost.
- **Cost:** S to M. The states exist in the patient portal (UC-23); the status and notification
  design is waiting on Daniel.
- **Evidence:**
  - 121Clinicians' late-call FAQ (confirmed today);
  - 95% contact rate with the branded number (meeting notes, line 87);
  - the COMP file's slot-model finding (still true).
  - Weaker than before: Avee's late-call reviews were not re-verified today.
- **Measure:**
  - booking conversion from the homepage;
  - missed-call rate;
  - share of patients reached on the first call;
  - review volume and rating mentioning "on time" or "kept me updated".
- **Risks:**
  - A visible queue shows bad days too. The honesty has to be real.
  - Estimated times conflict with Daniel's "queue number, not duration" (`8cd0893`); keep to position
    and state.
  - SMS content needs `privacy-security` review.

- **Impact:** medium. Reviews compound acquisition. Forms and ICBC work are uninsured, so priced

- **Cost:** S for reviews and the physician page; M for ICBC (process, templates, billing); L for SOC 2.
- **Evidence:**
  - Avee's and Maple's review counts;
  - Rocket's SOC 2 Type I badge;
  - 121's ICBC page;

- **Measure:**
  - Google review count and rating;

  - share of visits in "Ongoing Health".
- **Risks:**
  - ICBC reporting rules, fees and forms are not known to me. **Needs `billing-msp` and a qualified

### Bet 5: Open distribution channels the walk-ins don't own

- **What:**
  - **Pharmacies:** a pilot with independent BC pharmacies. Pharmacy refers → SimpleCare queue →
    the script faxed back to that pharmacy, with fax status (REQ from S1).
  - **AI assistants:** harden and promote the existing llms.txt, API and MCP so assistants recommend
    SimpleCare with live availability.
  - Later, **employers** for private-pay non-residents.
- **Impact:** medium, and long-term. Avee's 700 pharmacies and Rocket's device stations show
  pharmacies are a proven BC channel. The AI channel is new and nobody holds it.
- **Cost:** M for pharmacies (a partnership, a referral flow, an agreement); S for the AI channel.
- **Evidence:**
  - Avee (https://www.avee.health/);
  - Rocket Doctor (https://www.rocketdoctor.ca/);
  - simplecare.ca/llms.txt and /mcp.
- **Measure:**
  - visits by referral source;
  - referrals per pharmacy per week;
  - AI-referred sessions (tagged links in the API responses).
- **Risks:**
  - Pharmacy referral and steering arrangements may have professional-conduct rules for both
    physicians and pharmacists. **Needs a qualified reviewer.**
  - Avee already holds 700+ pharmacies.
  - AI answers can be wrong, so the API must stay read-only, as llms.txt already says.

**Not a top-5 bet yet: leaving BC.** Tia, Rocket and TELUS already serve Alberta. TELUS ended
Ontario episodic care on 8 Apr 2026, and Rocket charges privately for Ontario primary care, which
suggests Ontario's public funding for virtual walk-in care is hard. **Opinion:** win BC first. Treat
Alberta as the likely second province once section 7 is answered.

## 7. Regulatory and licensing questions for expansion (flagged, not asserted)

All of these are for a qualified reviewer (health lawyer, `billing-msp`, `privacy-security`). The
summaries below are what the sources appear to say. None is a legal conclusion.

1. **CPSBC Virtual Care standard (v9.3, 8 Apr 2026).** Per a search summary, it appears to require
   BC registrants to meet the licensing rules of the patient's jurisdiction. It also appears to
   require a pre-established arrangement for in-person care where the patient is located. Does
   SimpleCare meet the in-person arrangement today, inside BC? What does it need per province?
   https://www.cpsbc.ca/files/pdf/CPSBC-PS-Virtual-Care.pdf
2. **Attachment and LFP.** Can a virtual-only practice attach patients and bill under LFP? A search
   summary says LFP physicians provide care "in both in-person and virtual formats". Can SimpleCare
   receive patients from the Health Connect Registry?
   https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/health/practitioner-professional-resources/msp/physicians/longitudinal-family-physician-lfp-payment-model
3. **Canada Health Act Services Policy (effective 1 Apr 2026).** Law-firm commentary says patient
   or employer charges for medically necessary care by some regulated providers may count as user
   charges. What does this mean for SimpleCare's private pay (non-residents, uninsured services)?
   Does it help an MSP-first model against paid memberships?
   https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/04/2026-update-to-the-new-interpretation-of-the-canada-health-act,
   https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/news/2025/01/statement-from-the-minister-of-health-on-the-canada-health-act.html
4. **Ontario.** OHIP splits virtual care into "comprehensive" (an existing relationship) and
   "limited" (none), at lower fees. What counts as an existing relationship for a virtual practice?
   Is it viable, given the TELUS and Rocket retreat? Is PHIPA compliance needed, and AODA for the
   patient portal?
   https://www.ontario.ca/document/education-and-prevention-committee-billing-briefs/virtual-care-1-comprehensive-and-limited
5. **Alberta.** Secondary billing guides say Alberta virtual-care claims need an established
   relationship, with the patient seen in the previous 12 months. Tia and Rocket nonetheless say AB
   visits are free. Which is right today? What do CPSA registration and the Alberta Health
   Information Act require? https://cpsa.ca/physicians/standards-of-practice/virtual-care/,
   https://www.albertadoctors.org/practice/virtual-care/
6. **Licensure per doctor.** Does each doctor need a licence in each province where patients are
   located, and does CMPA coverage extend to it? Can MOAs in BC handle other provinces' records under
   those provinces' privacy laws?
7. **PharmaNet equivalents and lab feeds** outside BC, for the "results from the source" promise
   (ANS26:56). This is for `integrations-engineer`.
