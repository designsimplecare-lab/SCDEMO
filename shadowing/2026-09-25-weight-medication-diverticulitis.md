## Patterns across the four visits
- **The note is not written during the call, 4 out of 4.** Here today's note was on screen for about
  one second and was never touched.
- **The call is spent reading.** In shadowing 2 and 3 he read old notes. Here it was the old note first,
  then about four minutes in lab PDFs. What the visit needed (a weight history, recent metabolic labs,
  what happened with the earlier GLP-1) was not in any note.
- **A new problem in an established patient.** Shadowing 2 and 3 were follow-ups, where the last plan
  held the answer. This visit is a new reason (weight) for a patient with unrelated history. The latest
  note was the wrong place to look, and he read it anyway, because it is what the chart opens on.
- **Results are files, not data.** The labs are uploaded PDFs with generic names, all dated by their
  upload, unsorted, with duplicates. Finding one result means opening files one at a time. Nothing puts
  one test next to its earlier values (glucose, lipids, kidney function over time), and there is no
  weight trend at all, only the patient's own figure on the intake.
- **What he needs comes from outside.** Shadowing 1: other platforms. Shadowing 3: outside operative
  reports. Here: lab reports ordered by other physicians and uploaded in a batch.
- **Calling is fine 2 visits out of 4.** It was clean here and in shadowing 2. It went wrong in
  shadowing 1 and 3.
- **The action row goes unused on screen, 4 out of 4.** No MOA hand-off is visible here either.
- **Nested scrolling everywhere.** Here there were five scroll regions: the page behind, the chart
  modal, the left nav, the lab list, and the PDF viewer (with its thumbnail strip). Opening history or
  results pushes today's note out of view, as in shadowing 3.
- **Entry detours.** A stale search from the last patient, then intake in its own modal, then the chart.

## What v2 already covers
- **Calling and dropped calls.** One press dials, and a dropped call shows "Call dropped at … · Redial"
  (UC-03). The call went cleanly here, so there was nothing to fix.
- **An honestly empty note.** v2's note placeholder is "Document the visit..." and it starts from the
  intake reason as the chief complaint. The ear-pain example and the early "Note documented" stamp are
  production problems only.
- **Finalize closes the visit and locks the note.** Not reached in this recording.
- **Critical result at the top of the chart.** Not relevant here: nothing was critical, only flagged.
- **Results as data.** v2's Results tab lists tests as lines with a value and a flag (for example,
  "Ferritin 18 µg/L · Low"), not as PDFs. That already answers "which file is it?" for results that come
  in as data.
- **Partly covered: "Since last visit".** It exists, but it is built from the previous visit's plan. For
  a new reason like this one, it would show the iron-deficiency plan, which is the wrong content.
- **Not built yet: intake inside the chart and old notes beside today's note** (UC-05, UC-08). v2 still
  opens the intake as a separate view, and still has nowhere to read history without hiding today's note.

## What it changes in v2
- **When the reason is new, "Since last visit" says so.** If no earlier note touches today's reason, the
  block reads "First visit for weight" and shows that reason's context instead: the intake answers, the
  weight history, and the latest relevant labs. The unrelated last plan drops to a single line below.
- **Weight as a trend, not one number.** "Last vitals" shows a single weight today. For a weight reason,
  the chart shows the weight over time, marking each reading as patient-reported or measured, with the
  intake figure as the newest point. It shows BMI calculated from height and weight, and the change since
  the first reading.
- **Results by test, over time.** Every test in the Results tab shows its earlier values and dates in one
  line (for example, fasting glucose: three values, dated, the latest flagged), so trends are read without
  opening files. For a weight or diabetes reason, a preset group (glucose/A1c, lipids, kidney, liver, TSH)
  appears first.
- **Uploaded lab PDFs are named, dated and sorted by collection.** An uploaded report takes its
  collection date and the tests it holds as its title ("Aug 19, 2025 · CBC, chemistry, CRP"), not "Custom
  Lab" plus the upload date. The list sorts newest collection first. A file identical to one already on
  the chart is flagged as a duplicate. A cancelled-only report says "Cancelled by lab" in the row. The
  viewer's header names the report, never an internal file id. How much can be read from a scanned PDF
  is an open question (below).
- **Show the age of the latest labs.** If the most recent relevant result is more than a year old, the
  group says "Last drawn 13 months ago" and offers a prefilled requisition for the preset. This is a
  draft the doctor sends with one press, not an automatic order.
- **Keep the list where it was.** Closing a document viewer returns the list at the same scroll
  position, with that row highlighted. Show the view action always, not only on hover, and keep delete
  away from it (behind the row's menu).
- **The medication history answers the intake.** The intake says a GLP-1 was tried before. The chart
  should show when, what dose and why it stopped, from the medication list, or "not on file" if it was
  prescribed elsewhere. That way the doctor asks about the gap instead of hunting for it.
- **No stale search.** Opening the Daysheet clears a patient search from an earlier visit, or shows it
  as a filter chip with a clear button.
