Inbox and Doctor Action Centre become separate systems

Building
Doing
The structural change the rest of this depends on.

Inbox

Incoming external clinical information — LifeLabs results, imaging, consultation reports, discharge summaries.

Doctor Action Centre

Internal work — patient follow-up, prescriptions, forms, referrals, staff escalations, delegated tasks.

Rules
  • Reviewing an Inbox item can generate a linked Action Centre task. The document itself stays in the Inbox and the chart — it never moves.
  • Three views: My Actions, Team Actions, Completed.
  • Every task carries priority, time tolerance, deadline, assignee, status.
  • Priorities are Urgent, High, Routine. Overdue is a status, not a priority — overdue items sort first within their existing band.
Design note. Five metadata fields per row won't scan. Proposing: patient and action on the primary line, priority as a severity stripe, deadline as relative time ("in 2h", "3d overdue"), assignee as an avatar, and time tolerance only in the detail view.

Call windows and Carryover

Building
Doing
  • Default to the current call window, not All. Auto-advances through the day.
  • Unfinished patients from the previous window pin to a Carryover section at the top, never hidden.
  • On change, a brief notice: the new window started, and how many carried over.
  • Never interrupts an active call or an open chart.
  • Selecting a window manually pauses auto-advance.
  • In All view, time separators mark each window — 8:00–10:00, 10:00–2:00, 2:00–5:00, 5:00–7:00.
Changed from the brief
Auto is a persistent visible state — "Auto-advancing · 2:00–5:00" with one tap back — rather than one option buried in the dropdown. Otherwise doctors won't notice they've left it, which is the classic mode-confusion bug.
Open detail
A patient who carries over twice stays in Carryover with an age indicator rather than stacking a second section. Flagging it; not blocking.

Call button restricted to the active patient

Decision
Asked
Show the call button only on the next person in line. It mirrors a walk-in — nobody buds the line — and sequential calling is what makes the queue function.
Concern
This hides a control to enforce a policy. Patient 1 doesn't answer; patient 3 is a stroke callback. Reality will demand exceptions, and Add-On isn't one — it's a booking action, not a "call now" action.
Proposing
Keep the button on every row but visually secondary off the active patient, with a confirm: "Gloria Anne McDonald is next in line. Call Andrey anyway?" The norm holds, judgment is still possible, and the doctor learns the rule instead of finding a missing button.
Need from Daniel
Is sequential calling an absolute rule, or a strong default?

A Task button on every patient

Decision
Asked
A Task button per patient — in queue rows, in the chart, inside the Referrals, Imaging, Labs and Prescriptions modals, and on the day sheet. He notes it will look cluttered and left the solution to us.
Reading
The real requirement is that tasking carries patient context automatically — not that there are a dozen buttons.
Proposing
One persistent Task MOA button that pre-fills whichever patient is open or selected, plus a row-level action on hover and keyboard focus.
Need from Daniel
Confirm pre-filled context is the need. If he genuinely wants a visible button on every row, that's buildable — it just costs row density.

Care Plan summary in the Inbox

Decision
Asked
